# India's Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) Protests - 2026 Research Report

## Overview
The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) is a youth-led protest movement in India that emerged in mid-2026, becoming one of the most significant challenges to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Hindu nationalist government in recent years. The movement began as a satirical online campaign and evolved into mass street protests demanding education reform and government accountability.

## Origins and Name
- The CJP name originated from a remark by India's Chief Justice Surya Kant, who allegedly compared unemployed young people with "fake and bogus degrees" to cockroaches during a court hearing in May 2026.
- He later clarified he was referring specifically to people with fraudulent credentials, not India's youth broadly.
- The name is a parody of Modi's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).
- Founded by Abhijeet Dipke, a 30-year-old political communications strategist and former Boston University student.

## Key Trigger: NEET Exam Leak Crisis
- The National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) is India's main medical college entrance exam.
- On May 3, 2026, nearly 2.28 million candidates sat for the exam.
- Days later, the exam was cancelled after allegations of question paper leaks were confirmed by a government inquiry.
- All results were invalidated, forcing students to retake the exam under tight security.
- More than 20 students died by suicide due to distress after the exam cancellation, according to family claims.
- This was not an isolated incident - since 2014, at least two dozen exams had their results invalidated due to leaks.

## Protest Timeline
- May 2026: CJP founded as online satirical campaign
- June 2026: CJP members began sit-in at Jantar Mantar, Delhi
- June 28, 2026: Sonam Wangchuk (education activist) began hunger strike in support
- July 18, 2026: Delhi police forcibly removed and hospitalized Wangchuk, sparking national outrage
- July 20, 2026: Tens of thousands marched on parliament, met with police crackdown (tear gas, baton charges)
- July 20-25, 2026: Protests spread across India - Mumbai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Goa, Dehradun
- July 25, 2026: Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned
- July 25, 2026: CJP called off protests after achieving demands
- August 2026: Protests continued in Jharkhand over separate exam irregularities

## Key Demands
1. Resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan
2. Education system reform
3. Greater accountability over exam paper leaks
4. Compensation for families of students who died by suicide
5. New Public Examinations Act for transparency
6. Reform of examination bodies
7. Students' rights charter
8. Welfare fund for students and families
9. Parliamentary oversight of examination authorities

## Broader Grievances
- Youth unemployment: 16.2% among 15-29 year olds (June 2026), up from 13.8% a year earlier
- Fewer than 7% of jobseekers secured permanent salaried work within a year (2026 Azim Premji University study)
- Fewer than 4% landed white-collar jobs
- Only 15% of those trained under PM Kaushal Vikas Yojana skill program landed jobs in 10 years
- Widening economic inequality
- Erosion of free speech rights
- Indians under 30 are three times as likely as average citizen to be unemployed

## Key Figures
- Abhijeet Dipke: CJP founder, 30-year-old political communications strategist
- Sonam Wangchuk: Education activist, went on 26-day hunger strike
- Dharmendra Pradhan: Education Minister who resigned on July 25, 2026
- Narendra Modi: Prime Minister of India
- Rahul Gandhi: Leader of opposition Indian National Congress
- Sakhi: 22-year-old activist (they/them pronouns), created viral Instagram Reels
- Harshvardhan Kadam: 18-year-old whose police encounter video went viral (10 million views)

## Government Response
- Education Minister Pradhan resigned on July 25, 2026
- Modi announced fast-track courts for paper leak cases
- New law with harsher penalties for exam leaks proposed
- Panel created to review examination system
- Compensation promised for families of students who died by suicide
- Cases against protesters dropped in several cities
- Parliament hiked penalties for leaking exam papers

## Digital Organizing
- CJP used social media extensively (Instagram, X/Twitter)
- Government temporarily blocked CJP's X account in India
- Instagram/Meta began censoring protest-related content under government pressure
- Protesters used Instagram Reels to spread message
- AI-powered camera technology used by authorities to monitor protesters
- Internet shutdowns around protest sites in Delhi

## Opposition Response
- Congress party and INDIA bloc supported the movement
- Rahul Gandhi led protest outside Modi's residence
- Opposition MPs protested in Parliament complex
- Demand for Home Minister Amit Shah's resignation over police excesses

## Impact and Significance
- First significant concession by Modi government to public protest in years
- Only second cabinet resignation in Modi's 12+ years in power
- Exposed rising political influence of population under 25 (nearly half of India)
- Shift toward digital-first organizing
- Movement questioned BJP's governance ability rather than ideology
- Cut across caste, class, and gender lines
- Restored space for dissent and questioning in Indian politics

## Related Protests
- Jharkhand student protests (August 2026): Separate movement over JPSC/JSSC exam irregularities
- Farmers' protests (2021): Previous major protest movement that forced government to repeal agriculture laws
- Anti-corruption movement (2011): Historical comparison for digital mobilization
